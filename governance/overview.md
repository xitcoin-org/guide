# Governance and participation

Xitcoin governance is intended to make protocol decisions visible and verifiable while preserving clear operational responsibilities.

## Four distinct concepts

| Concept | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Governance | Propose and decide eligible protocol changes |
| Validator admission | Determine which operators may enter the validator process |
| Staking | Allocate economic weight to approved validators |
| Operations | Maintain software, infrastructure, keys and service continuity |

None of these automatically replaces the others. A large balance does not by itself grant administrative authority, and an approved operator must still satisfy staking and operational requirements.

## Public, institutional and sovereign participation

The long-term vision allows different categories of participants to connect to the same protocol, including public validators and potential sovereign or institutional operators.

The design objective is coexistence without hidden privilege:

* every validator follows published technical and security requirements;
* admission decisions are recorded on-chain;
* stake and voting power remain visible;
* reserved participation capacity does not silently become active power;
* no country, institution or public delegator receives an undocumented automatic right;
* parameter changes follow the applicable authority and governance process.

The final mainnet allocation between sovereign and public validator capacity remains a launch-policy decision. It must be published only when the on-chain maximum, approval model and economic strategy are consistent.

## Testnet governance

The testnet includes Cosmos governance infrastructure for exercising proposal, deposit, voting and tally behavior. Testnet decisions and balances do not create mainnet rights.

Before participating, query the active network for deposit, voting, quorum, threshold and veto parameters rather than relying on an older document.

## Accountability

A credible governance process should provide:

1. a clear proposal;
2. an identifiable authority or proposer;
3. a review period;
4. public parameters and expected effects;
5. an auditable result;
6. an implementation and rollback plan where relevant.
